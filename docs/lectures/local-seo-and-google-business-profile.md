Scene 1 — How local search works

```text
Pick up your phone and search for coffee near me. In under a second, Google shows you a map, three businesses and maybe an AI summary. Why those three? Why not the café you walked past this morning? ```
```

Scene 2 — Why it matters

```text
Why does this matter? For restaurants, clinics, salons, tradespeople and shops, local results are where customers decide. Calls, direction requests and bookings happen right there on the results page, often without anyone visiting a website. If you understand how local ranking works, you stop wasting money on myths, like stuffing keywords into your business name, and you focus on the levers that actually move.
```

Scene 3 — Local search surfaces

```text
First, what counts as local search? Any query where the searcher wants something nearby or in a specific place. Dentist near me. AC repair Dubai Marina. Best karahi in Lahore. Google answers with several features. The local pack, the map with a few listings. The local finder, when you tap more places. Google Maps itself. Organic results with local intent, like city service pages and directories. And increasingly, AI answers in Search and Maps that recommend places and summarise reviews. Most businesses need to show up in several of these, not just one.
```

Scene 4 — Google's three factors

```text
Here's the key idea. Google's own help documentation says local results are based mainly on three factors. Relevance: how well a profile matches what someone searched for. Distance: how far each business is from the location used in the search. And prominence: how well known the business is, based on information from across the web, like links, articles and directories, plus review count and ratings. Google also says your position in regular web results counts towards prominence. And there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.
```

Scene 5 — What you control

```text
An analogy helps. Imagine you ask a friend to recommend a plumber. They'll suggest someone who actually does the job you need. That's relevance. Someone close enough to come today. That's distance. And someone they've heard good things about. That's prominence. Google is doing the same thing at massive scale. Now, what can you control? Relevance, a lot: your categories, services and website. Distance, barely: it's your real address. Prominence, over time: reviews, local links, coverage and a strong website.
```

Scene 6 — Distance is normal

```text
Understanding distance is liberating. A business will naturally rank better near its location and worse far away. That's not a failure of your SEO. It's how local search works. And please, never try to fake proximity with a virtual office or a co-working address where you're not staffed. That typically violates Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended. For wider reach, you use organic pages on your website and, where it's legitimate, additional real locations.
```

Scene 7 — Example 1 (simple)

```text
Worked example one, simple. A bakery in Jumeirah, Dubai, ranks in the pack for bakery when someone searches nearby, but not from Dubai Marina. That's distance. To reach more customers, it perfects relevance with the primary category Bakery, a secondary category Cake shop and products for custom cakes. It builds prominence with steady reviews, local press about its date cakes, and reputable local listings. And it creates a custom cake ordering page on its website that can rank organically across the city.
```

Scene 8 — Example 2 (illustrative)

```text
Worked example two, realistic and illustrative. Dr Sana runs a dental clinic in Gulberg, Lahore. She's frustrated. The clinic doesn't show in the pack for dentist even when she searches from the waiting room. That's not distance. When a business is invisible right next door, suspect relevance or eligibility. We check: the primary category is set to Medical clinic, not Dentist. There's also a duplicate profile a former receptionist created years ago, splitting reviews between the two.
```

Scene 9 — The fix

```text
The fix: change the primary category to Dentist, add relevant secondary categories, and ask Google to merge or remove the duplicate using the proper support route. Then add services with short descriptions and link the profile to a proper dentist page on the website. Over the following weeks, and these results are illustrative, the clinic starts appearing in the pack near the clinic, and calls from the profile rise. Nothing was hacked. The basics were just wrong.
```

```text
If you could change only one thing on a Google Business Profile, what would have the biggest impact on which searches it appears for? For most businesses, the answer is the primary category. Scene 2 — Why it matters
```

```text
Why does this matter? Relevance is the factor you control most, and categories are the strongest relevance signal on the profile. The primary category also unlocks features, like booking buttons, menus and service lists, depending on the category. Choose it badly and you'll struggle to appear for your most valuable searches, however many reviews you collect.
```

Scene 3 — The key idea

```text
Here's the key idea. Your primary category tells Google what your business fundamentally is. Think of it like the sign above a shop in a busy market. People scan the signs to decide where to go. If a sweet shop's sign says General Store, customers looking for sweets walk straight past. You get one primary category and several secondary categories. Categories are fixed. You must choose from Google's list, which differs by language and country.
```

Scene 4 — Category method

```text
Here's a step by step method. List your core services and how customers search for them. Find the matching categories in Google's list. Research competitors: check the primary category of businesses ranking in the pack for your main queries. Pick the primary category that matches your main revenue service and the query you most want. Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely provide as a meaningful part of the business. Then change one thing at a time and measure over several weeks.
```

Scene 5 — Example 1 (simple)

```text
Worked example one, simple. A Lahore restaurant specialising in karahi, which also does catering. Primary category: Pakistani restaurant. Secondary: Caterer, Barbecue restaurant and Takeout restaurant, because it genuinely does all three. What it doesn't add: Bakery, just because it sells a few desserts, or Event venue, because it doesn't host events. Irrelevant categories dilute relevance and can be removed by Google anyway.
```


```text

Then services: add a service for each thing you do, with one to three sentences covering what it is, who it's for, a distinctive detail, and price guidance where sensible. Keep it consistent with the matching page on your website.
```

Scene 8 — Products + attributes


```text
Hours matter more than people think. If your hours are wrong, Google may show closed at the moment someone searches, and you'll lose open now searches and gain angry reviews. Set special hours for public holidays, Ramadan schedules, Eid, Christmas and New Year. Use more hours for things like delivery or drive through where available. And keep your website saying the same things: a page for each main service, the same name, address and phone, and LocalBusiness schema that matches the profile.
```


```text
Now the rules. Google's policies prohibit fake engagement: reviews that don't reflect a genuine experience, reviews written by the business or staff, and bought reviews. ```
```


Scene 3 — Grid tracking = a weather map

```text
Here's the key idea: grid based tracking. Think of it like a weather map. One thermometer at the airport doesn't tell you the temperature across the city. A grid of readings does. ```
```

```text
Set it up sensibly. Choose queries that reflect how customers search: core services, service plus area, and a few brand queries, including both near me style and explicit city queries. ```
```

