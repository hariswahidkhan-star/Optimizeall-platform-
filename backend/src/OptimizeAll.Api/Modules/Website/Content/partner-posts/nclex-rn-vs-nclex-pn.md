The NCLEX-RN and NCLEX-PN are both developed by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN), both use the Next Generation NCLEX (NGN) item formats and both assess whether a candidate is safe to begin practice. But they are not the same exam at two difficulty levels. They measure readiness for **different scopes of practice**, and the best preparation for each reflects that difference.

This guide explains how the exams differ, what that means for the way questions are framed, and a tailored study approach for each.

> Test plans, content weightings, administration rules and eligibility are set by the NCSBN and the nursing regulatory bodies, and they are updated periodically. Check the NCSBN's current NCLEX-RN or NCLEX-PN test plan and your regulatory body's requirements.

## Who takes which exam

- **NCLEX-RN** — for graduates of registered nurse programmes seeking licensure as a registered nurse.
- **NCLEX-PN** — for graduates of practical or vocational nursing programmes seeking licensure as a licensed practical or vocational nurse (LPN/LVN).

Your education programme determines which exam you are eligible for.

## The core difference: scope of practice

Scope of practice is set by law and regulation and varies by jurisdiction, but in broad terms:

| Area | RN focus | PN focus |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment | Comprehensive assessment and analysis | Data collection and contribution to assessment |
| Care planning | Develops and revises the plan of care | Contributes to and follows the plan of care |
| Delegation and supervision | Delegates, supervises and coordinates care | Works under the direction of an RN or other licensed provider; may assign tasks within scope |
| Client teaching | Develops and delivers teaching | Reinforces teaching |
| Complex or unstable clients | Primary responsibility for clinical judgement | Recognises changes and reports them promptly |

This difference runs through the questions. An RN item might ask what the nurse should do after analysing a deteriorating client's data; a PN item on a similar client might focus on recognising the change and reporting it appropriately.

## How the test plans differ

Both test plans organise content around the same broad client-needs categories:

- **Safe and Effective Care Environment**
- **Health Promotion and Maintenance**
- **Psychosocial Integrity**
- **Physiological Integrity**

The subcategories and their emphasis differ. For example, within Safe and Effective Care Environment, the NCLEX-RN test plan includes **Management of Care**, while the NCLEX-PN test plan includes **Coordinated Care** — reflecting the difference between managing and coordinating care versus contributing to it. Pharmacology content is framed differently too: the RN plan includes pharmacological and parenteral therapies, while the PN plan focuses on pharmacological therapies within practical nursing scope.

Always check the current test plan for the exact subcategories and percentage ranges.

## The same NGN formats on both

Both exams use NGN item types: unfolding case studies, bow-tie and trend items, and response formats such as multiple response, matrix, drop-down, drag-and-drop and highlight. The clinical judgment steps — recognise cues, analyse cues, prioritise hypotheses, generate solutions, take action, evaluate outcomes — apply to both, but the *expected action* reflects the scope of practice. Our guide to [NGN question types](/blog/nclex-ngn-question-types) explains the formats in detail.

## A worked contrast

*A client with heart failure has gained 1.5 kg since yesterday, has new crackles in both lung bases and reports needing more pillows to sleep.*

- **RN-framed question:** *Which actions should the nurse take?* Expected reasoning: recognise the cues of worsening fluid overload, analyse them in the context of heart failure, notify the provider, anticipate and implement ordered interventions, monitor response, and update the plan of care.
- **PN-framed question:** *Which findings should the practical nurse report to the registered nurse or provider?* Expected reasoning: recognise the weight gain, crackles and orthopnoea as changes from baseline, report them promptly, and continue to collect data and implement care within the plan.

Same client, same knowledge base, different expected role. Practising with the wrong frame can lead you to choose actions outside your scope — or to under-act. (This is a study illustration only, not clinical guidance.)

## Study approach for NCLEX-RN

**Emphasise:** prioritisation, delegation and supervision, management of care, complex and unstable clients, and evaluating outcomes.

An eight-week outline at around 15 hours a week:

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read the test plan; diagnostic practice set; build an error log |
| 2 | Management of care: prioritisation, delegation, legal and ethical issues |
| 3 | Safety and infection control; pharmacological and parenteral therapies |
| 4 | Physiological adaptation and reduction of risk potential |
| 5 | Health promotion; psychosocial integrity |
| 6 | NGN case studies across content areas; bow-tie and trend practice |
| 7 | Mixed timed practice; error-log review |
| 8 | Full practice session, targeted review, rest |

## Study approach for NCLEX-PN

**Emphasise:** data collection, recognising and reporting changes, coordinated care, working within the plan of care, safety, and reinforcing teaching.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read the test plan; diagnostic practice set; build an error log |
| 2 | Coordinated care: scope of practice, working with the healthcare team, client rights |
| 3 | Safety and infection control; pharmacological therapies |
| 4 | Basic care and comfort; reduction of risk potential |
| 5 | Physiological adaptation; health promotion and psychosocial integrity |
| 6 | NGN case studies across content areas with a focus on recognising and reporting |
| 7 | Mixed timed practice; error-log review |
| 8 | Full practice session, targeted review, rest |

## Practising delegation and assignment questions

Delegation and assignment items catch many candidates on both exams, for opposite reasons: RN candidates sometimes keep tasks they should delegate, while PN candidates sometimes accept tasks outside their scope. A simple routine for these items:

1. **Identify the client's stability.** Stable clients with predictable outcomes are generally more suitable for assignment to other team members than unstable or newly admitted clients.
2. **Identify the task type.** Tasks involving initial assessment, care planning, evaluation and complex teaching usually remain with the RN; routine, well-defined tasks with predictable outcomes can often be assigned within the team.
3. **Match the task to the team member's scope and competence**, as defined by regulation and facility policy.
4. **Check supervision and communication.** Who will the team member report changes to, and what should they report?

Practise by taking a list of five clients and five tasks and assigning each, then justifying every assignment in one sentence. Do the same exercise from the PN perspective: which assignments would you accept, and which would you question? Because scope rules vary by jurisdiction, anchor your answers in the principles tested by the NCSBN test plan rather than in local custom.

## Habits that help for both

- **Label the scope.** Before answering, ask: "What is *this* nurse's role here?"
- **Label the clinical judgment step** the item is testing.
- **Justify every selection** from the case data, especially where partial scoring applies.
- **Space your review** of lab values, medication classes and priority frameworks — see [Spaced repetition and active recall for professional exams](/blog/spaced-repetition-and-active-recall-for-exams).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the NCLEX-PN easier than the NCLEX-RN?

It is better thought of as different rather than easier. Each measures readiness for its own scope of practice, using its own test plan.

### Can I use NCLEX-RN materials to study for the NCLEX-PN?

Some content overlaps, but RN materials frame many questions around RN responsibilities such as delegation and care planning. Use materials written for your exam so you practise the correct role.

### Do both exams use Next Generation NCLEX items?

Yes. Both use NGN item types, including case studies, bow-tie and trend items.

### How do I know which exam I should take?

Your nursing education programme determines your eligibility. Check with your programme and your regulatory body.
