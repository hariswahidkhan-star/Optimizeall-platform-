PCI AI offers three certifications for people who plan, control, finance and lead projects: **PCL-AI** (Project Controls Leader), **PFL-AI** (Project Finance Leader) and **PML-AI** (Project Management Leader – AI). They share one exam format, so much of the preparation is common — but each has a distinct body of knowledge, and the best study plan reflects that.

This guide sets out the facts, a shared preparation method, an eight-week plan for each credential, and technique for scenario-based questions.

## The facts that shape your preparation

According to [PCI AI](https://pciai.org), each certification:

- has a **90-minute** exam, taken **fully online**;
- is **scenario-based**, with AI governed throughout;
- requires **65%** to pass;
- has a **USD 350** exam fee;
- is **valid for three years**, with recertification rules published on PCI AI's site.

PCI AI describes PCL-AI as open to anyone with three years' experience, in any field. Before you start studying, read the eligibility requirements, exam structure, body of knowledge and sample questions on PCI AI's site — they are the authoritative sources, and your plan should map directly to them.

What the facts imply for study:

- **Ninety minutes is not long.** Scenario questions take reading time. Speed comes from fluency with concepts, not from rushing.
- **Scenario-based means applied.** Expect to choose the best action or interpretation in a situation, not just recall a definition.
- **AI is governed throughout.** Expect scenarios that test when AI use is appropriate, how outputs should be validated and who is accountable — in every domain, not in a separate chapter.

## The shared method: MAP-PRACTISE-REVIEW

**1. MAP (week 1).** Turn the official body of knowledge into a checklist. Rate each topic: 0 = new to me, 1 = know it, can't apply it confidently, 2 = apply it at work. Your study time goes to the 0s and 1s.

**2. PRACTISE (weeks 2–7).** For each topic: learn the concept, work a numerical or scenario example by hand, then answer scenario-style questions under light time pressure.

**3. REVIEW (continuous).** Keep an error log: question topic, why you got it wrong (concept gap, misread, calculation slip, poor option elimination), and the fix. Revisit it weekly. Spaced review beats rereading — see [Spaced repetition and active recall for professional exams](/blog/spaced-repetition-and-active-recall-for-exams).

## PCL-AI study plan: Project Controls Leader

**Scope:** planning, cost engineering, earned value, forecasting, risk and project finance with the governed use of AI.

| Week | Focus | Practice task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Map the body of knowledge; baseline self-assessment | Rate every topic 0/1/2 |
| 2 | Planning and scheduling logic, critical path, float | Build a 30-activity network and find the critical path by hand |
| 3 | Cost engineering: estimating, cost breakdown, basis of estimate | Write a basis of estimate for one work package |
| 4 | Earned value: PV, EV, AC, CPI, SPI, EAC, TCPI | Work three reporting periods by hand ([worked example](/blog/earned-value-management-explained)) |
| 5 | Forecasting with ranges; risk registers and quantitative risk basics | Turn a single-point EAC into a range with stated drivers |
| 6 | Project finance fundamentals: CFADS, coverage ratios | Calculate DSCR and LLCR on a small cash flow ([guide](/blog/dscr-vs-llcr-vs-plcr)) |
| 7 | Governed AI across the control cycle | Write an approved-use note and review checklist for AI forecasts |
| 8 | Mixed timed practice and error-log review | Two timed sets; fix the top three error patterns |

## Technique for scenario-based questions

Use the **READ-RULE-RANK** routine:

1. **READ the last line first.** Know whether you are asked for the *best* action, the *first* action, the most likely *cause* or the correct *calculation* before reading the scenario.
2. **RULE out options that break a principle.** Options that bypass governance, ignore accountable people, use AI outputs without validation or change a baseline without approval are usually wrong, however efficient they sound.
3. **RANK the remaining options.** Prefer actions that gather evidence before acting, address root causes rather than symptoms, and involve the right decision-maker.

For calculation questions, write the formula first, then substitute. Most errors in earned value and coverage-ratio questions come from swapped terms (EV ÷ AC versus AC ÷ EV) rather than arithmetic.

## The final week

- Do not learn new topics in the last three days; consolidate.
- Take one full timed practice session at the same time of day as your exam.
- Check your online exam setup — computer, connection, quiet room, identification — well before exam day, following PCI AI's instructions.
- Review your error log, not your whole notes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which PCI AI certification should I take first?

Choose by role. PCL-AI suits planners, schedulers, cost engineers and controls leads; PFL-AI suits people in project finance, modelling, lending or PPP roles; PML-AI suits project and programme managers leading delivery. Check PCI AI's certification roadmap and eligibility requirements.

### How long should I study for a PCI AI exam?

It depends on your experience with the body of knowledge. The eight-week plans above assume around five to seven hours a week for someone with relevant work experience; add time for topics you rate as new.

### Are the PCI AI exams open book?

Check PCI AI's exam structure and candidate rules for the current conditions. Prepare as if you will rely on your own understanding, because scenario questions reward fluency.
