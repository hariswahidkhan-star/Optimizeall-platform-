The Certified Management Accountant (CMA) credential, awarded by the Institute of Management Accountants (IMA), is built for accountants and finance professionals who plan, analyse, control and support decisions inside organisations. Its exam has two parts, and each combines multiple-choice questions with essay scenarios — so a plan that only drills multiple choice leaves marks on the table.

This guide gives you a system that works for both parts: how to map the content, a 12-week schedule per part, essay technique with a worked example, and a review routine.

> Content outlines, question formats, timing, scoring, fees, testing windows and eligibility rules are set by the IMA and can change. Check the IMA's current requirements and the official content specification before you plan.

## The two parts at a glance

- **Part 1 — Financial Planning, Performance, and Analytics.** External financial reporting decisions, planning and budgeting, performance management, cost management, internal controls, and technology and analytics.
- **Part 2 — Strategic Financial Management.** Financial statement analysis, corporate finance, decision analysis, risk management, investment decisions and professional ethics.

Parts can be taken in either order. Many candidates start with the part closest to their day job, to build momentum.

## Step 1: map the content specification

Download the official content specification and turn it into a tracker with three columns:

| Topic | Rating (new / rusty / strong) | Planned weeks |
|---|---|---|

Weight your time by the topic weightings in the specification *and* your rating. A heavily weighted topic you rate as new gets priority; a lightly weighted topic you already know gets a quick review.

## Step 2: a 12-week plan per part

At around 12–15 hours a week, a structure like this works for many working candidates. Adjust weeks to the weightings in the current specification.

**Part 1 example**

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | External financial reporting decisions |
| 3–4 | Planning, budgeting and forecasting |
| 5–6 | Performance management: variance analysis, responsibility accounting |
| 7–8 | Cost management: costing systems, overhead allocation |
| 9 | Internal controls |
| 10 | Technology and analytics |
| 11 | Mixed practice and two essay sets |
| 12 | Full timed practice exam, error-log review, rest |

**Part 2 example**

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Financial statement analysis: ratios, limitations |
| 3–4 | Corporate finance: risk and return, capital structure, working capital |
| 5–6 | Decision analysis: CVP, marginal analysis, pricing |
| 7 | Risk management |
| 8–9 | Investment decisions: capital budgeting, NPV, IRR |
| 10 | Professional ethics |
| 11 | Mixed practice and two essay sets |
| 12 | Full timed practice exam, error-log review, rest |

Within each week: learn on two weekdays, practise multiple choice on two weekdays, and at the weekend do a mixed set plus one essay scenario.

## Step 3: master the essay scenarios

Essays test whether you can explain and apply, not just calculate. Candidates often lose marks by producing a correct number with no explanation, or a long explanation that never answers the question.

Use the **C-A-L-C** routine:

1. **C — Clarify** the requirement. Underline the verbs: *calculate*, *explain*, *recommend*, *identify*.
2. **A — Answer** each requirement in its own labelled section.
3. **L — Lay out** calculations with labels and formulas, so partial credit is possible even if a number is wrong.
4. **C — Conclude** with a recommendation where one is asked for, linked to your numbers.

### Worked example: a make-or-buy decision

*Scenario:* A company makes 10,000 units of a component a year. Costs per unit: direct materials 12, direct labour 8, variable overhead 4, allocated fixed overhead 6. A supplier offers the component at 27 per unit. If the company buys, 40% of the allocated fixed overhead would be avoided, and the freed capacity could be rented out for 20,000 a year. **Calculate the financial effect of buying and recommend a course of action, noting two qualitative factors.**

*Answer:*

```
Relevant cost to make (per unit):
  Direct materials         12
  Direct labour             8
  Variable overhead         4
  Avoidable fixed (40%x6)   2.4
  Total                    26.4

Annual cost to make      = 26.4 x 10,000 = 264,000
Annual cost to buy       = 27.0 x 10,000 = 270,000
Less rental income                        (20,000)
Net cost to buy                           250,000

Financial advantage of buying = 264,000 - 250,000 = 14,000 per year
```

*Recommendation:* On financial grounds, buy — saving about 14,000 a year, **provided** the rental income is reliable. *Qualitative factors:* supplier quality and delivery reliability; loss of in-house capability and control; effect on the workforce.

Note the structure: the unavoidable 60% of fixed overhead is excluded because it is not relevant, the opportunity (rental) is included, and the recommendation is conditional on stated assumptions. That is exactly what markers look for.

## Step 4: multiple-choice technique

Multiple-choice questions make up most of each part, so small improvements in technique compound. A routine that works:

1. **Read the last sentence first.** Know whether you are asked for a cost, a variance, a ratio or a judgement before you read the data.
2. **Predict before you look.** Estimate the answer or its direction (favourable or unfavourable, higher or lower) before scanning options. Distractors are designed around common errors, so a prediction protects you.
3. **Watch for relevance traps.** Sunk costs, allocated fixed costs and non-cash items often appear in the data precisely because they should be excluded.
4. **Flag and move on.** If a calculation is taking more than about twice your average time per question, make your best choice, flag it and return later.
5. **Review flagged questions with fresh eyes**, starting with the ones you felt closest to solving.

### Variance analysis: a quick check

Variance questions are a favourite source of avoidable errors. Before calculating, write the direction you expect: if actual price is above standard price, the price variance is unfavourable. Then compute, for example:

```
Material price variance = (Actual price - Standard price) x Actual quantity
                        = (5.20 - 5.00) x 10,000 = 2,000 unfavourable
```

If your result's direction does not match your prediction, recheck the formula before choosing an answer.

## Step 5: a review system that sticks

- **Error log** for every missed question and essay point: topic, error type (concept, calculation, misread, incomplete explanation) and the fix.
- **Formula and concept cards** reviewed on a spaced schedule — see [Spaced repetition and active recall for professional exams](/blog/spaced-repetition-and-active-recall-for-exams).
- **Weekly mixed sets** so earlier topics stay fresh.
- **One essay per week, timed**, marked against a model answer.

## Step 6: the final fortnight

- Take a full timed practice exam 10–14 days out and analyse results by topic.
- Spend most remaining time on the two or three weakest topics and your most common error type.
- In the last two days, review your error log and cards only.
- Practise your essay routine one final time so it runs automatically on the day.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which CMA part should I take first?

Either order is allowed. Many candidates start with the part closest to their daily work to build confidence, then tackle the other while study habits are strong.

### How important are the essays?

Important enough to practise every week. Essays test explanation and application, and structured answers earn partial credit even when a calculation slips.

### How long does it take to prepare for each CMA part?

It depends on your background and weekly study time. Map the official content specification, rate your familiarity with each topic, and plan realistic weekly hours; the 12-week templates above are a starting point.

### Do I need work experience to become a CMA?

The IMA sets education and experience requirements for certification. Check the IMA's current rules, including when experience can be completed relative to the exam.
